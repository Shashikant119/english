ENGLISH GRAMMAR: COMPLETE TENSE GUIDE
=====================================

Basic terms
-----------
Subject: The person or thing that performs the action.
Examples: I, you, he, she, it, we, they, Rahul, the customer

V1: Base form of a verb
Examples: work, go, write, update

V2: Past form of a verb
Examples: worked, went, wrote, updated

V3: Past participle form of a verb
Examples: worked, gone, written, updated

Verb + ing: Present participle
Examples: working, going, writing, updating

Important short forms
---------------------
I am = I’m
You are = You’re
He is = He’s
I have = I’ve
He has = He’s
I will = I’ll
Do not = Don’t
Does not = Doesn’t
Did not = Didn’t
Will not = Won’t
Cannot = Can’t


THE 12 ENGLISH TENSES
=====================

1. SIMPLE PRESENT TENSE
-----------------------
Use:
- Habits and routines
- General facts
- Regular or repeated actions
- Permanent situations

Hindi पहचान:
ता है, ती है, ते हैं

Structure:
Positive: Subject + V1(s/es) + object
Negative: Subject + do/does not + V1 + object
Question: Do/Does + subject + V1 + object?

Use “s” or “es” with he, she, it, or a singular name.

Examples:
- I update the website every day.
- He updates the product page every day.
- I do not update the website on Sunday.
- Does she check the orders every morning?
- The sun rises in the east.

Common mistake:
Incorrect: He update the website.
Correct: He updates the website.


2. PRESENT CONTINUOUS TENSE
---------------------------
Use:
- An action happening right now
- A temporary action
- A planned near-future action

Hindi पहचान:
रहा हूँ, रही है, रहे हैं

Structure:
Positive: Subject + am/is/are + V1-ing + object
Negative: Subject + am/is/are not + V1-ing + object
Question: Am/Is/Are + subject + V1-ing + object?

Examples:
- I am updating the product page.
- She is checking the website.
- They are not working on the collection page.
- Are you testing the new feature?
- We are meeting the client tomorrow.

Common mistake:
Incorrect: I am update the page.
Correct: I am updating the page.


3. PRESENT PERFECT TENSE
------------------------
Use:
- A recently completed action
- A past action whose result is important now
- Life experience without mentioning an exact past time
- An action completed at an unspecified time

Hindi पहचान:
चुका हूँ, चुकी है, किया है, हुआ है

Structure:
Positive: Subject + has/have + V3 + object
Negative: Subject + has/have not + V3 + object
Question: Has/Have + subject + V3 + object?

Use “has” with he, she, it, or a singular name.
Use “have” with I, you, we, and they.

Examples:
- I have updated the offers on the product page.
- She has fixed the mobile menu.
- We have not completed the work yet.
- Have you checked the changes?
- The developer has resolved the issue.

Common mistake:
Incorrect: I have updated it yesterday.
Correct: I updated it yesterday.

Reason:
Use Simple Past with a finished past time such as yesterday, last week, or in 2025.


4. PRESENT PERFECT CONTINUOUS TENSE
-----------------------------------
Use:
- An action that started in the past and is still continuing
- A recently stopped action whose effect is visible now
- To emphasize the duration of an action

Hindi पहचान:
से कर रहा हूँ, से काम कर रही है

Structure:
Positive: Subject + has/have been + V1-ing + since/for
Negative: Subject + has/have not been + V1-ing + since/for
Question: Has/Have + subject + been + V1-ing + since/for?

Since = starting point
Examples: since Monday, since 10 a.m., since 2024

For = duration
Examples: for two hours, for three days, for a year

Examples:
- I have been working on this website for two hours.
- She has been testing the feature since morning.
- We have not been receiving orders since Monday.
- Have you been waiting for a long time?

Common mistake:
Incorrect: I am working here since 2024.
Correct: I have been working here since 2024.


5. SIMPLE PAST TENSE
--------------------
Use:
- A completed action in the past
- An action connected to a finished past time
- A sequence of completed past actions

Hindi पहचान:
किया, गया, आया, हुआ

Structure:
Positive: Subject + V2 + object
Negative: Subject + did not + V1 + object
Question: Did + subject + V1 + object?

Examples:
- I updated the product page yesterday.
- She fixed the issue last night.
- We did not change the design.
- Did you test the website?
- I opened the file, checked the code, and fixed the error.

Common mistake:
Incorrect: Did you checked the page?
Correct: Did you check the page?

Reason:
Always use V1 after “did.”


6. PAST CONTINUOUS TENSE
------------------------
Use:
- An action that was in progress at a specific past time
- A continuing past action interrupted by another action
- Two actions happening at the same time in the past

Hindi पहचान:
रहा था, रही थी, रहे थे

Structure:
Positive: Subject + was/were + V1-ing + object
Negative: Subject + was/were not + V1-ing + object
Question: Was/Were + subject + V1-ing + object?

Use “was” with I, he, she, it, or a singular name.
Use “were” with you, we, and they.

Examples:
- I was updating the page at 5 p.m.
- They were testing the website.
- She was not working when I called her.
- Were you checking the mobile view?
- I was writing the code while he was testing it.


7. PAST PERFECT TENSE
---------------------
Use:
- An action completed before another action in the past
- To show which of two past actions happened first

Hindi पहचान:
कर चुका था, कर लिया था, हो गया था

Structure:
Positive: Subject + had + V3 + object
Negative: Subject + had not + V3 + object
Question: Had + subject + V3 + object?

Examples:
- I had completed the update before the client called.
- She had left before I reached the office.
- We had not tested the page before publishing it.
- Had you checked the code before uploading it?

Timeline:
First: I completed the update.
Second: The client called.

Combined:
I had completed the update before the client called.


8. PAST PERFECT CONTINUOUS TENSE
--------------------------------
Use:
- An action that continued for some time before another past action
- To emphasize the duration of a past activity

Hindi पहचान:
से कर रहा था, से काम कर रही थी

Structure:
Positive: Subject + had been + V1-ing + since/for
Negative: Subject + had not been + V1-ing + since/for
Question: Had + subject + been + V1-ing + since/for?

Examples:
- I had been working for three hours before the system stopped.
- She had been testing the website since morning.
- They had not been receiving orders for two days.
- Had you been waiting for a long time before I arrived?


9. SIMPLE FUTURE TENSE
----------------------
Use:
- Future decisions
- Promises and offers
- Predictions
- Actions that will happen in the future

Hindi पहचान:
करूँगा, करेगी, करेंगे

Structure:
Positive: Subject + will + V1 + object
Negative: Subject + will not + V1 + object
Question: Will + subject + V1 + object?

Examples:
- I will update the page tomorrow.
- She will check the website.
- We will not change the layout.
- Will you review the changes?
- I will fix the issue as soon as possible.

“Going to” for plans or strong evidence:
- I am going to update the product page tonight.
- Look at those clouds. It is going to rain.


10. FUTURE CONTINUOUS TENSE
---------------------------
Use:
- An action that will be in progress at a specific future time
- A polite question about someone’s future plan

Hindi पहचान:
कर रहा होऊँगा, कर रही होगी, कर रहे होंगे

Structure:
Positive: Subject + will be + V1-ing + object
Negative: Subject + will not be + V1-ing + object
Question: Will + subject + be + V1-ing + object?

Examples:
- I will be working on the website at 5 p.m.
- She will be testing the feature tomorrow morning.
- We will not be using the old design next week.
- Will you be attending the meeting?


11. FUTURE PERFECT TENSE
------------------------
Use:
- An action that will be completed before a specific future time

Hindi पहचान:
कर चुका होऊँगा, कर चुकी होगी, कर चुके होंगे

Structure:
Positive: Subject + will have + V3 + object
Negative: Subject + will not have + V3 + object
Question: Will + subject + have + V3 + object?

Examples:
- I will have completed the update by 6 p.m.
- She will have tested the website by tomorrow.
- We will not have finished the project by Monday.
- Will you have reviewed the page by tonight?

Important:
By 6 p.m. = completed before or at 6 p.m.


12. FUTURE PERFECT CONTINUOUS TENSE
-----------------------------------
Use:
- An action that will continue up to a specific point in the future
- To emphasize future duration

Hindi पहचान:
से कर रहा होऊँगा, से काम कर रही होगी

Structure:
Positive: Subject + will have been + V1-ing + since/for
Negative: Subject + will not have been + V1-ing + since/for
Question: Will + subject + have been + V1-ing + since/for?

Examples:
- By 6 p.m., I will have been working for eight hours.
- Next month, she will have been working here for two years.
- Will you have been studying for three hours by 9 p.m.?


QUICK COMPARISON
================

Present:
- I update the website every day. (habit)
- I am updating the website now. (happening now)
- I have updated the website. (recently completed; result matters now)
- I have been updating the website for two hours. (started earlier and still continuing)

Past:
- I updated the website yesterday. (completed past action)
- I was updating the website at 5 p.m. (in progress at a past time)
- I had updated the website before the meeting. (completed before another past action)
- I had been updating the website for two hours before the meeting. (duration before another past action)

Future:
- I will update the website tomorrow. (future action)
- I will be updating the website at 5 p.m. (in progress at a future time)
- I will have updated the website by 6 p.m. (completed before a future time)
- I will have been updating the website for two hours by 6 p.m. (duration up to a future time)


IMPORTANT GRAMMAR RULES
=======================

1. Use of “a,” “an,” and “the”
--------------------------------
A:
Use before a singular, non-specific noun beginning with a consonant sound.
- I found a problem.
- I added a banner.

An:
Use before a singular, non-specific noun beginning with a vowel sound.
- I found an issue.
- It took an hour.

The:
Use for a specific person, thing, place, or idea.
- I fixed the issue you reported.
- I updated the offers on the product page.
- Please check the changes.

First mention:
- I found an issue.

Second mention:
- I fixed the issue.


2. Use of “since” and “for”
---------------------------
Since + starting point:
- since Monday
- since morning
- since 2024

For + duration:
- for two hours
- for three days
- for one year


3. Use of “has” and “have”
--------------------------
Has: he, she, it, singular name
- She has updated the page.

Have: I, you, we, they
- I have updated the page.
- They have checked the website.


4. Use of “do,” “does,” and “did”
---------------------------------
Do: I, you, we, they
- Do you check the website daily?

Does: he, she, it, singular name
- Does she check the website daily?

Did: all subjects in the past
- Did they check the website yesterday?

Always use V1 after do, does, or did.
- Does he work here? Correct
- Does he works here? Incorrect


5. Common time words
--------------------
Simple Present:
always, usually, often, sometimes, every day

Present Continuous:
now, right now, at the moment

Present Perfect:
already, just, yet, ever, never, recently

Simple Past:
yesterday, last week, ago, in 2025

Future:
tomorrow, next week, soon, later


PRACTICE EXERCISES
==================

Fill in the blanks:

1. I ______ the product page every day. (update)
2. I ______ the product page right now. (update)
3. I ______ the product page already. (update)
4. I ______ the product page yesterday. (update)
5. I ______ the product page tomorrow. (update)
6. She ______ the issue before the client called. (fix)
7. They ______ the website for two hours. (test)
8. By 6 p.m., I ______ the work. (complete)

Answers:

1. update
2. am updating
3. have updated
4. updated
5. will update
6. had fixed
7. have been testing
8. will have completed
